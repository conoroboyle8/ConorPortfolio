Composer Profile

Music Built Around Story, Texture, And Form

Conor O'Boyle is an Irish composer, producer, and orchestrator from Derry whose work spans film, television, concert music, and contemporary classical writing.

Rooted in the expressive traditions of Romanticism while embracing contemporary approaches to harmony, colour, and musical form, his work is characterised by lyrical melody, rich harmonic language, and a strong sensitivity to texture.

Whether writing for the concert hall or the screen, he seeks to create emotionally resonant musical narratives that balance craftsmanship with a distinctly modern voice.

Background

Screen, Stage, And Studio

A graduate of Newcastle University, Conor received a BA (Hons) in Music before completing a Masters degree in Scoring for Film and Visual Media at Pulse College, Windmill Lane Studios, Dublin.

His music has been heard in screen projects including The Tested, After The Bomb, BBC documentary series Murder In The Badlands, Netflix's HYFIN, and TG4 work.

His concert music has been performed at venues including Sage Gateshead, London's Southbank Centre, and The MAC Belfast, and he has collaborated with And So I Watch You From Afar on the multi-platform project Jettison.

Fantasia For Strings & Harp

A richly lyrical orchestral work that reimagines the expressive colours of the Romantic orchestra through a contemporary lens.

Written for string orchestra and harp, the work unfolds as a fluid musical landscape in which delicate textures gradually expand into moments of broader orchestral warmth.

A wonderful piece, full of colour, lyricism, harmony, and with his individual stamp clearly on the music.

Nan Schwartz, Grammy-winning composer
Watch Video Listen On Spotify

Programme Note

Fantasia for Strings & Harp explores the expressive potential of the string orchestra through a continuously unfolding musical structure. Muted strings and harp establish an atmosphere of suspended stillness before fragments of melody surface within gently shifting harmonies.

The harp acts both as a colouristic partner and a subtle structural guide, illuminating the harmonic landscape as the music moves between chamber-like delicacy and broader orchestral resonance.

Recorded at Studio One of Bulgarian National Radio in Sofia by the European Recording Orchestra, conducted by Maestro Christo Pavlov.
